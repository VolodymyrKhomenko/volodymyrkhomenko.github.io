Headline results

📝Survey analysis in hours, not days

A large team satisfaction and process-gap survey runs three times a year. I analyse the responses with AI, which cut the analysis from several days to a couple of hours and pinpointed which current processes were not working. I then use AI to build the presentation for leadership, which helped focus attention and effort on closing the critical gaps.

🎙️From voice notes to a clickable prototype

I recorded the requirements for a new tool by voice, turned them into technical documentation with AI, and built a clickable prototype that aligned the developers and became the starting product backlog. Requirements documentation went from about a week to a couple of hours, and the handover to developers from weeks to a few days.

💬AI chatbot rolloutprojected savings

I coordinated the testing and rollout of an AI chatbot as one of three early-adopter business units, and acted as key user and trainer for the tool. Each of the 20 initiative leaders spends 2 to 4 hours a week on a calculation of potential scrap and non-productive inventory. The chatbot is projected to cut at least half of that, saving ~20 to 40 hours per week across the group. It also moves the calculation from weekly to daily, so teams react faster to customer orders, material changes, and deliveries.

How I use AI every day

AI-powered, human-centered. AI is my thinking partner. It drafts documents, presentations, and messages for me, and it also questions my ideas, challenges my assumptions, and shows me blind spots. I decide what goes out, and what goes out still sounds like me.

Tools. At work I use Microsoft 365 Copilot, the AI approved for the company environment. For personal projects I use ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, and Claude and ChatGPT are the two I use the most. In chat I analyse data, extract key information from complex documents, and build knowledge bases and process documentation.

Beyond chat, for personal projects I use agentic tools such as Claude Cowork and ChatGPT Work, where the AI carries out multi-step tasks from a shared brief instead of a back-and-forth. In Claude Cowork I run my own working system: a command center with standing rules, memory files, and a separate workspace for each area of my work, so every session starts with the right context. I build my own Skills in Claude, which are reusable instructions for tasks I repeat, and I connect Claude to my other tools with connectors and plugins.

My command center and my voice. That command center also holds a full memory of who I am, what I stand for, what I stand against, and how I write. Everything I produce starts from that, so it keeps my voice.

Context over clever prompts. I do not rely on clever prompts. I believe in context engineering: the more context I give an AI, the better the result. I explain the purpose, the goal, the value, the format I need, and the change I want to achieve.

Flipped interaction and voice. I mostly let the AI interview me, one question at a time, so it learns more about my work and about me. I answer by voice with Superwhisper, a dictation app, because talking is much faster than typing and gives the AI richer context.

Visual pages. I think visually, so I often ask AI to build an interactive, clickable HTML page instead of a document. It makes information easier to take in. This portfolio is an example.

I use NotebookLM for learning. It works from the sources I upload and shows where each answer comes from, so I can check conclusions against the original. For visual tasks I use Claude Design and Canva, and Lightroom and Photoshop for photography.

How I keep it reliable

Sources first. I give AI my own documents to work from, and every figure traces back to a source document. This page is an example: I checked every number on it against my CVs and LinkedIn profile with a script.

Ethics. I never put company-sensitive data into AI tools that are not approved for it. I check every result myself before I use it, I treat AI answers as drafts to challenge, and I challenge my own thinking too.
